WATERWISE - SHORT PRESENTER GUIDE
=================================

PROJECT TITLE
AI-Assisted Wastewater Monitoring and Anomaly Detection

60-90 SECOND INTRODUCTION
-------------------------
"Our project uses Python, data analysis and unsupervised machine learning to
screen wastewater-treatment measurements for unusual patterns. We selected
the UCI Water Treatment Plant dataset, which contains 527 observations and
38 numeric variables collected from an urban wastewater-treatment plant.

We first convert the measurements to numeric values and handle missing
readings using median imputation. We then standardize the variables and apply
Isolation Forest to identify unusual combinations of measurements.

In Saketh's run, the model flagged 27 observations, or 5.12%, as unusual.
We also separately compare final-effluent BOD, COD and suspended solids with
their dataset-specific 90th percentiles. The dashboard visualizes these
measurements, shows the anomaly scores, and provides investigation suggestions
such as checking aeration, reviewing organic loads and inspecting clarification.

The purpose is to help prioritize further investigation. The flags are not
proof of a pollution incident or a regulatory violation."

LIKELY FACULTY QUESTIONS
------------------------
Q: What is Isolation Forest?
A: An unsupervised anomaly-detection algorithm. It isolates unusual data
   points through random feature and split selection. It does not need a
   labelled normal/polluted class for training.

Q: Why did you choose it?
A: We did not have verified incident labels for each observation. Isolation
   Forest can screen multivariable data for unusual combinations without
   requiring those labels.

Q: What does contamination=0.05 mean?
A: It configures the model to expect roughly 5% of observations to be unusual.
   It is a modelling setting, not a pollution percentage or legal threshold.

Q: Why are accuracy, precision and recall not shown?
A: There is no ground-truth label for every observation in this experiment,
   so supervised classification metrics cannot be calculated meaningfully.

Q: What do BOD, COD and SS mean?
A: BOD is the oxygen demand from biological breakdown of organic matter. COD
   is the oxygen equivalent of chemically oxidizable substances. SS means
   suspended solids in the water.

Q: Does an anomaly mean the water is unsafe or illegal to discharge?
A: No. It only means the combination of measurements is unusual relative to
   the dataset. Operators must verify the readings and compare them with the
   applicable discharge standards.

Q: Is this connected to live sensors?
A: No. It analyses historical measurements from the public UCI dataset. A
   live sensor connection would be a future extension.

Q: How are the recommendations produced?
A: They combine two separate signals: Isolation Forest anomaly flags and
   Python rules that check whether selected effluent values exceed the
   dataset-specific 90th percentile. The recommendations are rule-based
   investigation prompts, not direct outputs of the ML model.
